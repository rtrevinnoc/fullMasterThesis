How the Paper Handles the "Rest State" of Each Spring

Al-Rawashdeh, Al Janaideh & Heertjes — Mechanism and Machine Theory 170 (2022) 104638

The teacher's question: Hooke's law says the restoring force is K · (displacement from rest), not K · (total displacement). The paper writes K · f in the equations of motion with no explicit nominal subtracted. So where is the nominal (rest) position of each spring, and how is it accounted for?

1. The Chain — Two Kinds of Stages, Two Kinds of Springs

RED springs = FLEX stages — physical bearing/flexure. Rest is always f=0 (bodies touching at mount). f is a tiny vibration delta. GREEN springs = ACTUATED stages — electromagnetic motor. f can be 100 mm. "Rest" is defined as f=0 in open-loop, but u cancels that term.

2. Side-by-Side: How Each Type Handles f=0 as the "Rest"

FLEX Stage (i = 3, 5, 7 — odd)

Example: Long Stroke Flex (LS bearing, m₃ = 49 kg)

  • Physical reality: The bearing is an actual material connection between the motor carriage and the driven mass.
  • Rest state: When the motor is not accelerating, the bearing is un-stretched. The paper defines this as f₃ = 0. This is the true physical zero-stretch position.
  • Force law: F = K₃ · f₃ is exact Hooke's law because the nominal is genuinely 0. No correction needed.
  • Magnitude of f₃: Nanometers to micrometers — only inertial loading during acceleration causes this stretch.
  • Role of f₃: It IS the delta from nominal. Summing all flex f's gives the total vibration-induced positional error chain.

ACTUATED Stage (i = 2, 4, 6 — even)

Example: Long Stroke motor (m₂ = 0.5 kg)

  • Trajectory tracking: The stage doesn't just "sit" at a rest; it follows a time-varying reference f_d(t) (e.g., an 800 mm/s scan).
  • Model vs. Reality: In the math, f is the distance from the parent. In reality, the motor is "fighting" the virtual spring K·f to reach that position.
  • Feedforward (u_{ff}): To move 100mm against a stiffness K, the controller calculates K · f_d in advance and applies it. This pre-cancels the spring load.
  • Feedback (u_{fb}): Any residual difference e (tracking error) is corrected by the PID, which acts like an additional high-stiffness spring pulling the motor back to the ghost target.
  • Key takeaway: The large f is represented in the model to ensure the absolute position sum Σf is correct, but it is "neutralized" by the control force so it doesn't behave like a physical spring.

K·f never causes a net unbalanced physical force as long as u tracks it.

4. Why the Model Writes K·f (not K·(f − f_nominal))

Key insight: The paper uses K · f — with no subtracted nominal — because the model defines f = 0 as the rest state for every spring by construction. This is valid for two different reasons depending on the stage type:
  • Flex stages: f=0 is the genuine physical rest (un-stretched bearing). Hooke's law is K·f with no correction.
  • Actuated stages: f=0 is a modelling convention. The controller u absorbs the K·f_nominal term, so the model never needs to distinguish "nominal" from "delta" — the trajectory feedforward in u does this implicitly.
The model can write the same equation form (K·f) for all stages precisely because the actuated stages have a controller that ensures the K·f_nominal term is always cancelled.

5. Absolute Position: How Summation Still Works

Equation (2.1) of the paper gives the absolute position of stage i in the inertial frame:

f0i = f0i−1 + fi   →   f0wafer = f1(frame) + f2(LS motor) + f3(LS flex) + f4(SS motor) + f5(SS flex) + f6(wafer flex)

f₂ (LS motor) — large

f₂ ≈ 100 mm scan stroke.
This is the commanded large displacement. It correctly accumulates the full motor position into the absolute sum.

f₃ (LS flex) — tiny

f₃ ≈ a few nm.
This is the bearing stretch (delta from rest). It contributes the vibration error to the total absolute position.

Combined

The sum gives the true absolute position: large motor stroke + small bearing error. Both terms are needed and their different physical origins are exactly why the chain alternates actuated/flex stages.

Answer to the teacher's question in one sentence:
The paper writes K · f with no subtracted nominal because: for flex stages, f=0 is the genuine physical rest (the spring is literally un-stretched when f=0); for actuated stages, the control force u implicitly carries the feedforward term u ⊇ K · f_trajectory that cancels the mathematical K·f spring, so the motor never actually exerts the enormous force K·100mm — that term cancels algebraically before reaching the physical actuator.
